I am a Ph.D. researcher working on paleoclimate dynamics, hydroclimate variability, and model–proxy comparisons across Heinrich Stadials and orbital to millennial timescales.

My research combines climate-model experiments, proxy synthesis, and quantitative analysis to investigate how greenhouse gases, ice sheets, meltwater forcing, and orbital changes shape tropical and Asian monsoon precipitation.

Research

A few core themes that structure my current work in paleoclimate reconstruction and climate-model analysis.

Heinrich Stadial hydroclimate

Investigating tropical and Asian monsoon precipitation responses during Heinrich events using proxy networks, transient simulations, and targeted forcing experiments.

Model–proxy comparison

Evaluating consistency between simulations and paleoclimate archives with agreement metrics, uncertainty quantification, and regional synthesis.

Single-forcing experiments

Isolating the roles of orbital forcing, greenhouse gases, ice sheets, and freshwater forcing in shaping precipitation and ocean circulation.

Climate dynamics and mechanisms

Exploring moisture budget changes, AMOC variability, tropical circulation shifts, and inter-basin teleconnections across glacial climates.

About

I am a doctoral researcher interested in paleoclimate dynamics, transient simulations, monsoon hydroclimate, and the physical mechanisms behind abrupt climate change.

My recent work integrates global and regional climate models with paleoclimate archives, emphasizing moisture-budget diagnostics, forcing decomposition, and rigorous model–data evaluation.
