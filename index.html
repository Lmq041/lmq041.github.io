Biography

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Earth and Life Institute (ELI) at UCLouvain in Belgium. I received my Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees in Geoscience from Southwest University and National Taiwan University, respectively. My research focuses on the dynamics of Earth’s climate system, with particular emphasis on abrupt climate change and its hydroclimatic impacts.

My current work investigates the spatial and temporal diversity of climate responses during six Heinrich events of the last glacial period over East Asia. I develop an integrated framework combining global and regional climate models with vegetation modeling to examine how different external forcings—such as insolation, atmospheric CO₂, ice sheets, freshwater—and internal climate feedbacks shape regional climate variability. By integrating climate simulations with syntheses of paleoclimate proxy records and statistical analysis, my research aims to better understand the mechanisms driving past climate variability.

Research

Asian–Australian Monsoon–ENSO Coupling

High-resolution speleothem records reveal strong ENSO–monsoon coupling across multiple timescales during the last millennium.

Orbital Forcing and Hydroclimate Diversity during Heinrich Events

Climate model simulations show that orbital forcing can generate contrasting precipitation responses across the Asian monsoon region during Heinrich events.

Forcing Mechanisms of Tropical Hydroclimate

Combining climate models and proxy synthesis to quantify the roles of insolation, greenhouse gases, ice sheets, and freshwater in shaping hydroclimate variability.
